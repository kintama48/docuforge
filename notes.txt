3. Bring Your Own Template (BYOT) for PDFs
While you should not touch email templates, allowing BYOT for the PDF generation is an absolute, mandatory requirement.

Your /generate endpoint must accept two different payload structures:

Method A: The Managed Template
The developer passes a template_id and a data JSON object. You pull the Typst code from your Turso database and render it. This is for static, recurring documents like monthly invoices.

Method B: The Raw Injection (BYOT)
The developer passes a typst_string and completely bypasses your database.

This second method is your moat for the AI market. AI agents do not log into dashboards to configure templates. An autonomous agent will write the Typst code dynamically in memory and execute a POST request containing the raw code. If you force users to only use templates saved in your UI, you completely lock yourself out of the AI agent ecosystem.

Furthermore, strict engineering teams want to store their templates in their own Git repositories, not your database. By allowing them to send raw Typst code directly from their backend, they can version-control their templates locally and just use DocuForge as a dumb, hyper-fast compiler.
